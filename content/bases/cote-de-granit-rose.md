## Verdict

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-verdict" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-nature-first,evidence:northern-granit-rose-lannion-anchor" -->
The Côte de Granit Rose is the shortlist's strongest nature-first northern choice. It is defined less by a single historic city than by landscape — pink granite formations, the GR34 customs path, weather-beaten headlands, sandy family beaches, and island birdlife. Perros-Guirec, Trégastel, and the rail anchor Lannion function together as the base.

## Climate

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-climate-normals" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-climate" -->
This is one of the coolest-feeling northern options: typical August maxima around 20 degrees Celsius at Perros-Guirec (about 20 / 15 degrees Celsius), very low heat risk on the exposed shore, and roughly five rainy days in an August pattern. These are long-run August averages, not a forecast for 2026 — check a current forecast before you travel.

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-sea-normals" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-climate,evidence:northern-sea-temperature-north-coast" -->
The sea is roughly 16 to 17.5 degrees Celsius in August — refreshing rather than warm, suited to short swims and play. Wind on the open headlands makes the coast feel fresher still; sheltered coves feel noticeably warmer than exposed points.

## Swimming and beaches

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-beaches" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-beaches,evidence:northern-granit-rose-tregastel-aquarium" -->
Trestraou at Perros-Guirec is the full-facility sandy beach, while Coz-Pors at Trégastel is dotted with pink boulders and sheltered from the open ocean. In Trégastel, a small marine aquarium built directly into pink-granite caves offers a compact, weather-proof stop.

## What to do

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-ploumanach-walk" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-ploumanach-gr34" -->
The signature walk runs from Ploumanac'h along the GR34 past the pink rocks to the Mean Ruz lighthouse. Children enjoy scrambling over the granite boulders and reading animal shapes into them; short, manageable sections suit a family that does not want heavy mileage.

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-science-mix" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-science-mix" -->
The area's distinctive strength for a 9 to 10-year-old is a rare science-and-nature combination. The Parc du Radôme groups the Cité des Télécoms (advertised at over four hours of family visit), the 360-degree Planétarium de Bretagne, a reconstructed Gallic village reopening after renovation in 2026, and the nearby Vivons Perchés rope park. Confirm the Village Gaulois opening status before visiting.

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-sept-iles" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-sept-iles" -->
From Perros-Guirec, boat excursions run to the Sept-Îles archipelago, a seabird reserve that is one of France's largest Atlantic puffin sites. Landings are restricted, so most visits are boat-based viewing.

## Car trade-off

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-car-tradeoff" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-logistics-car" -->
A car markedly improves this base: the beaches and viewpoints are dispersed along local roads. The classic combination is a flight into Brest (with the direct Porto summer flight) plus a train to Lannion of about 1 h 15 min, or via Nantes (about 3 h 14 min). A car-free stay is feasible from Perros-Guirec, Lannion, or Trégastel but less convenient. Schedules are seasonal — re-check them before booking.

## Accommodation

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-accommodation" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-accommodation" -->
Accommodation is usually described as somewhat cheaper than Saint-Malo, though sea-view properties climb steeply in peak season. Indicative August ranges are apartments at €120 to €220 per night, family hotels at €110 to €260, and gîtes at €450 to €1,000 per week; a stone cottage is quoted around €900 to €1,400 per week. Book ahead for sea-view places — August is peak season.

## Food

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-food" sources="evidence:northern-granit-rose-food" -->
Food is reliable if less concentrated than Saint-Malo or Morbihan: Lannion's large Thursday market, many crêperies, and small seafood restaurants support a calm family evening of beach, a short walk, crêpes, and an early night.

## Linked area: Paimpol and Bréhat

<!-- paragraph id="granit-rose-paimpol-brehat" sources="evidence:northern-paimpol-brehat-linked-area" -->
West of the granite coast, Paimpol and the Île de Bréhat make a linked day-trip area rather than a seventh base: a working fishing port, a boat crossing to a car-free flower island, and a quieter coastal contrast to Perros-Guirec. Boat schedules to Bréhat are seasonal.

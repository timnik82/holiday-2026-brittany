## Verdict

<!-- paragraph id="crozon-verdict" sources="evidence:western-crozon-wild-coast" -->
The Crozon peninsula, with Douarnenez, is the shortlist's strongest pick for genuine wild Brittany: rocks, capes, geology, open ocean, a strong end-of-the-land feel, and an especially refreshing August. Pen-Hir, the Cap de la Chèvre, Morgat, and geological trails make the zone exceptionally strong on nature, while the Maison des Minéraux adds a family geology layer.

## Climate

<!-- paragraph id="crozon-climate-normals" sources="evidence:western-crozon-climate,evidence:western-brest-climate" -->
Crozon shares Finistère's freshest, most oceanic conditions: very low heat risk, with the corresponding trade-off of wind and changeable rain, and a cool sea around 16 to 18 degrees Celsius. It suits a family that packs layers and plans around the weather. These are long-run August conditions, not a forecast — check a current forecast and sea state before you travel.

## What to do

<!-- paragraph id="crozon-pen-hir-cap-chevre" sources="evidence:western-crozon-wild-coast,evidence:western-maison-des-mineraux" -->
The signature outdoor days run between dispersed viewpoints: the clifftop heath and rocks of Pen-Hir, the wild paths of the Cap de la Chèvre, and the GR34 trail linking coves and headlands. The Maison des Minéraux interprets the peninsula's geology and is a good family stop for a child who likes to understand how landscapes are made.

<!-- paragraph id="crozon-morgat-beach" sources="evidence:western-morgat-beach" -->
Morgat has a golden sandy beach set in an enclosed bay, where kayaks and paddleboards can be rented — the most sheltered family swimming spot in the area.

<!-- paragraph id="crozon-ile-vierge" sources="evidence:western-ile-vierge-restricted" -->
Plage de l'Île Vierge is famed for turquoise, Caribbean-like water, but access to the beach is restricted for safety reasons. It is best admired from the GR34 rocky path above rather than treated as a normal swimming beach — check local access guidance before attempting any descent.

## Car trade-off

<!-- paragraph id="crozon-car-tradeoff" sources="evidence:western-crozon-logistics-car" -->
The base's weak point is logistics. For a car-free family it is a compromise, because the point of the area is to drive between dispersed viewpoints. The sensible approach is to fly into Brest (from Porto) or come via Quimper and then rent a car. For an easy, low-effort first Brittany trip, Crozon is better as a second Brittany than a first; but if landscape, cool air, and wild beaches are the priority, it is very strong.

## Accommodation

<!-- paragraph id="crozon-accommodation" sources="evidence:western-crozon-accommodation" -->
Crozon and Morgat are usually pricier than Brest itself but often cheaper than Saint-Malo or Carnac at a comparable class. Indicative August ranges are apartments at €100 to €220 per night, family hotels at €120 to €220 per night, and gîtes at €380 to €900 per week. Book ahead — August is peak season.

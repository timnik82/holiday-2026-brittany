## Climate normals (not a forecast)

<!-- paragraph id="plan-weather-overview" sources="evidence:practical-climate-overview,evidence:brittany-august-air-temperature" -->
August in Brittany gives long days without Mediterranean heat: average daytime maxima of 20 to 24 degrees Celsius, cool nights of 12 to 14 degrees, and rare heat waves thanks to the Atlantic. This is exactly why the family is choosing Brittany over Portugal in August. These figures are long-run August climate normals, not a forecast for 2026 — check a current forecast before you travel.

<!-- paragraph id="plan-weather-daylight" sources="evidence:practical-daylight-hours" -->
Daylight in mid-August runs about 14 h 25 to 14 h 30 on the north and west coasts, and about 14 h 15 in the southern Morbihan — enough for a full morning-to-evening day at a relaxed pace.

## The microclimate split

<!-- paragraph id="plan-weather-microclimate" sources="evidence:practical-climate-microclimate-split" -->
Brittany's microclimates divide sharply. The north-east and south coast sit in milder coastal zones, while western Finistère is wetter and windier on average. Practical effect: the west is the freshest choice for a heat-averse family, while the [Morbihan](/bases/vannes-carnac-morbihan) south coast runs marginally warmer and drier — better for water time but a slightly higher heat risk.

## Changeable weather

<!-- paragraph id="plan-weather-variability" sources="evidence:practical-weather-variability" -->
Even in August, Brittany's weather is changeable: a sunny morning can turn to sea fog, drizzle or wind on the same day, especially on the exposed west and north coasts. Plan one flexible rainy-day activity per base (an aquarium, a museum, a covered market) rather than treating any day as guaranteed sun. The [Grand Aquarium](/things-to-do/grand-aquarium) at Saint-Malo and [Océanopolis](/things-to-do/oceanopolis) at Brest are the signature rainy-day anchors.

## Sea temperature

<!-- paragraph id="plan-weather-sea" sources="evidence:sea-temperature-wide,evidence:sea-temperature-narrow" -->
August sea temperatures along the Brittany coast usually range 16 to 19 degrees Celsius — refreshing rather than warm, suited to short swims and shallow play rather than long bathing. The Morbihan gulf runs marginally warmer (up to about 19 degrees); the exposed west and north coasts sit at the cooler end. A shorty wetsuit helps a child who tolerates cool water poorly.

## What to pack

<!-- paragraph id="plan-weather-packing" sources="evidence:practical-packing" -->
Pack in layers for changeable weather: a light rain shell and wind layer (essential on the exposed west and north coasts), swimwear and a shorty wetsuit for a child if needed, sturdy walking shoes for coastal paths like the [GR34 at Ploumanac'h](/things-to-do/ploumanach) and [Cap de la Chèvre](/things-to-do/crozon-pen-hir), and sun protection for the long daylight hours. On the north coast, the single most useful planning item is a printed or offline tide table for your stay — tides govern island causeway walks and beach safety.

## Why we are here

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-overview" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-overview" -->
The Gulf of Morbihan is the gentlest stretch of the trip, and the part the research agrees on most readily: the warmest and calmest bathing water in Brittany, sandy beaches, boats to small islands and a slower rhythm. It is described as the option that suits a child who tires on long transfers — which is why it takes the longest block of nights.

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-pace" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-pace,evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
The sources treat this region as something to stretch rather than tick off: beaches, an island, a little history and the megaliths, easily filling nine or ten days without becoming a race. Their own allocation is about four nights around Vannes and three or four around Carnac. We compress that into five nights on one base, which buys a genuine reserve day.

## Getting here

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-arrival" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-transfers,evidence:routes-entry-airports" -->
We come down from Nantes, the primary gateway from Portugal, and this is the lightest transfer of the whole trip. The airport shuttle to Gare Sud takes about 25 minutes, then the train Nantes–Vannes runs from 1 h 12 min — one of the softest door-to-destination scenarios in Brittany. By car it is roughly 1.5 hours to the Morbihan coast. Schedules are seasonal; re-check before travelling.

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-car" sources="evidence:routes-car-required-flexible" -->
A car is recommended rather than essential. Vannes and Carnac are walkable and rail-connected, but the megalith sites, Suscinio, the Quiberon peninsula and the island ferries are all far easier to reach with one.

## One base, no internal move

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-base-plan" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-sequencing,evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
The sources sequence this region as Vannes first — old town, walls, market, island boats, Suscinio — and Carnac second, for the megaliths, the Grande Plage and an oyster lunch. We collapse that into a single base and reach Carnac as a day trip, so no travel day falls inside these nights. The sources offer exactly this as the lower-move alternative for a family that prefers to stay put.

## Day by day

### Sunday 9 August — arrival and the old town

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-day-1" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-transfers,evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
Down from Nantes and settle in. An evening loop of the [medieval old town, walls and port](/things-to-do/vannes) is enough for a first day. The market on Place des Lices is worth timing into the week rather than forcing today.

### Monday 10 August — an island in the gulf

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-day-2" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
A boat to [Île-aux-Moines](/things-to-do/ile-aux-moines) or [Île d'Arz](/things-to-do/ile-d-arz): rented bikes, quiet lanes and small coves. If the weather closes in, swap to a sheltered gulf beach and a crêperie evening in Vannes.

### Tuesday 11 August — Suscinio and the Rhuys peninsula

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-day-3" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
Out to [Château de Suscinio](/things-to-do/suscinio) and the beaches of the Rhuys peninsula — a medieval castle on the gulf with sandy swimming close by. It is the clearest castle-and-beach pairing of the stay, and the easiest day to split between the two adults' and the child's interests.

### Wednesday 12 August — Carnac megaliths and the Grande Plage

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-day-4" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-sequencing,evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
Morning at the [Carnac alignments and the Maison des Mégalithes](/things-to-do/carnac-alignments), afternoon on the Grande Plage. Wet-weather swap: the [Parc de Branféré](/things-to-do/branfere), a family eco-park and animal reserve nearby.

### Thursday 13 August — Quiberon, or something quieter

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-day-5" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
The [Quiberon peninsula](/things-to-do/quiberon) — wild coast on the ocean side, sheltered coves on the bay side. The lighter alternative is an oyster lunch at [Auray / Saint-Goustan](/things-to-do/auray-saint-goustan) and a short walk through the market.

### Friday 14 August — on to Quimper

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-moving-on" sources="" -->
Check out and move west to Quimper. This crossing is not covered by the research corpus, so treat the timing as something to confirm on the day rather than a figure taken from a source. Nothing else is scheduled for the afternoon.

## The reserve day

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-reserve" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-pace,evidence:routes-relaxed-stay-lengths" -->
One day is deliberately left loose. The sources build this region around a reserve: an extra beach, an oyster lunch for the adults, a crêperie, a short market or souvenir stop. With five nights and no internal move, that reserve is real rather than theoretical — any day above can slide into it.

<!-- paragraph id="stay-vannes-inland" sources="evidence:routes-relaxed-inland-extension" -->
For an inland contrast there is the [Brocéliande forest at Paimpont](/things-to-do/broceliande) — the legendary forest of Merlin, with the Merlin-themed La Porte des Secrets — and [Lac de Trémelin](/things-to-do/lac-de-tremelin) for a freshwater swim. This leg has thinner support in the research than the coastal days; treat it as optional and lower-confidence.

# Best family-friendly beaches in Brittany for August 2026
## Overview
Brittany offers many safe, well-equipped beaches that work very well for families with a 10‑year‑old, though the Atlantic water remains relatively cool in August, typically between about 16–19 °C depending on location. The warmest sea areas in Brittany in August are around Saint‑Malo and Cancale (up to roughly 19 °C), while more western, exposed coasts such as Portsall are a few degrees colder. With your priorities (nature, historical towns, coastal scenery, quality of experience over quantity of spots), the most suitable family beaches cluster around the Emerald Coast (Saint‑Malo/Dinard), Pink Granite Coast (Perros‑Guirec area), Quiberon Bay/Carnac–La Trinité, and selected coves in North Brittany.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
## Key factors for the ranking
The ranking below considers:

- Safety: lifeguarded zones in July–August, gentle slopes, sheltered bays, presence of tidal pools.[^2][^3][^6]
- Water and waves: relatively warmer sectors (north/east Brittany), calmer waters or clear information about strong tides.[^5][^7]
- Infrastructure: toilets, showers, snack bars/restaurants, kids’ beach clubs, accessibility for families.[^3][^6][^8][^2]
- Parking and access: proximity of parking to the sand, reasonable access with a child and beach gear.[^9][^10][^3]
- Overall experience: landscape quality, nearby towns and activities, reviews highlighting family-friendliness.[^1][^10][^2]
## Top family beaches ranking
### 1. Grande Plage du Sillon (Saint‑Malo, Emerald Coast)
Plage du Sillon is a 3‑km sweep of fine sand along Saint‑Malo, repeatedly voted one of the most beautiful beaches in France and highlighted in recent guides to the best beaches near Saint‑Malo. Parts of the beach are lifeguarded in summer and the town provides extensive accessible‑beach infrastructure, including ramps, toilets, and free beach wheelchairs along several Saint‑Malo beaches in July–August, which is valuable with a child and for carrying gear. The beach has a gentle slope, good space even at high tide, and is backed by a promenade, cafés and easy access from a historic walled town, making it ideal to combine beach time with cultural visits.[^11][^8]
### 2. Plage de Bon‑Secours (Saint‑Malo)
Bon‑Secours, just below the old town ramparts, is famous for its 1937 seawater tidal pool, which creates a protected “piscine” for safe swimming at various tide states — very attractive for families with children. Recent family‑oriented beach guides list Bon‑Secours among the best beaches near Saint‑Malo for young children, precisely because of this pool, relatively calm water and the proximity to amenities. Coupled with the Saint‑Malo accessible‑beach program, you get lifeguards in season, facilities, and an easy walk to the old town for lunch or a museum visit.[^11][^8]
### 3. Kervillen Beach & Men Dû (La Trinité‑sur‑Mer, Quiberon Bay)
Local tourism and campsite guides describe Kervillen as a “gem” of a family‑friendly beach, around 800 m long, with Blue Flag water quality, lifeguards, parking and nearby bars, restaurants and accessible facilities. Men Dû, also in La Trinité‑sur‑Mer, is a more wild but still family‑oriented beach with gently shelving sand and calm waters inside Quiberon Bay, often compared to a large paddling area at lower tides for children. The surrounding Morbihan/Quiberon area receives positive reports from family travellers for quiet beaches, easy parking and a balance of sheltered coves and more open stretches for older kids who enjoy waves.[^2][^12][^6][^10]
### 4. Grande Plage de Carnac (Carnac, Morbihan)
Carnac’s main beach (Grande Plage) is described as several kilometres of fine sand with calm waters, lifeguard presence in peak season and a very family‑oriented atmosphere. The town and beach have Belle Époque‑style beach huts, kids’ clubs such as “Club Marsouins”, Blue Flag certification for water quality, and easy access to cafés, toilets and parking closer to the beach. For your trip, Carnac has the advantage of combining this beach with the famous Carnac standing stones and nearby coastal villages, giving both strong family beach time and high‑quality historical/natural excursions.[^13][^6][^10]
### 5. Plage de Trestraou & Saint‑Guirec (Perros‑Guirec, Pink Granite Coast)
On the Pink Granite Coast, several beaches around Perros‑Guirec are repeatedly recommended for families, including Trestraou (a popular family beach) and Saint‑Guirec. Trestraou offers fine sand, turquoise water and a range of nautical activities (paddleboarding, sailing) backed by restaurants and a promenade, which is convenient with a child. Saint‑Guirec is a sheltered cove facing the Costaérès islet, supervised in season and equipped with showers, restaurants and free parking, with easy access and gentle conditions for family swimming and sand play.[^2][^4][^14]
### 6. La Banche & Moulin Beach (Binic/Étables‑sur‑Mer, Bay of Saint‑Brieuc)
Family‑oriented accommodation providers in North Brittany highlight several beaches around Binic and Étables‑sur‑Mer as particularly child‑friendly. La Banche has a seawater pool and paddling area, lifeguard presence in July–August, and summer clubs and activities for children. Moulin beach in Étables‑sur‑mer is specifically described as “particularly child and baby‑friendly”, with lots of parking very near the sand, easy access for strollers, and bars and restaurants on the beach, making logistics with a 10‑year‑old straightforward.[^3]
### 7. Casino & Châtelet Beaches (Saint‑Quay‑Portrieux)
Casino and Châtelet beaches are two fine‑sand beaches separated by a sea‑water swimming pool and paddling area, and they are presented as perfect for families with young children. In summer they have lifeguards, a beach club, paddle/canoe hire and easy access to cafés, restaurants, a carousel and mini‑golf, compressing many family amenities into a walkable area. This sector of North Brittany also offers good coastal paths and viewpoints for short hikes with a child.[^3]
### 8. Men Du & other Carnac/La Trinité beaches at low tide (natural paddling pools)
Men Du beach appears in several guides as a gently sloping beach that at low tide turns into a vast natural paddling pool, which is excellent for children discovering the ocean for the first time. Though Men Du itself is not always lifeguarded, the lagoon‑like conditions at low tide and the ability to play safely in shallow water with buckets and nets are repeatedly highlighted for families. Nearby beaches such as Ty Bihan and Légenèse provide variations in atmosphere and exposure, with some calmer and more secluded options while still close to Carnac’s services.[^2][^6]
### 9. Rosaires Beach (Plérin, Bay of Saint‑Brieuc)
Rosaires is described as a 2‑km fine sandy beach, considered “the beach of the Briochins” (Saint‑Brieuc residents), with supervised swimming in summer, a promenade, restaurants and shops. The wide sandy area and lifeguard supervision make it suitable for families, and the bay setting often means less powerful surf than on the fully exposed Atlantic headlands. For your purposes, it is a strong option if you pass through the Saint‑Brieuc region en route between Saint‑Malo/Dinan and the Pink Granite Coast.[^13][^3]
### 10. La Salinette (Saint‑Briac‑sur‑Mer, Emerald Coast)
La Salinette is an extra‑fine sand, supervised beach lined with white bathing cabins and overlooked by the Nessay castle, recommended as an authentic, small but well‑equipped beach suited to short family swims between visits. It offers showers and toilets and is accessible to people with reduced mobility, which generally implies ramps and an easy approach with a child carrying toys and towels. The nearby Emerald Coast villages and coastal paths add scenic walks to the beach time.[^2]
## Water temperature, waves and tides in August
In August, Brittany’s water is cool but swimmable for most children with brief immersions, wetsuits or at least short sessions: long‑term observations show average maxima below 20 °C, with the warmest areas (Cancale, Saint‑Malo bay) around 19.4 °C, and colder exposed areas like Portsall around 15–16 °C. This makes the Emerald Coast and enclosed Morbihan/Quiberon Bay more comfortable for longer swims than the far‑west Atlantic coast, which is better for older kids who enjoy waves but may require shorter dips. Brittany also has very large tidal ranges; official tourism materials explain that coefficients above 100 mean extremely high tidal ranges, with the sea receding far and returning very high, so families should check tide tables and be cautious when exploring mudflats or rocks.[^5][^7][^9][^10]
## Practical recommendations
### General advice for a family with a 10‑year‑old
- Prioritize lifeguarded beaches during July–August and look for Blue Flag labels (Carnac, Kervillen, parts of Saint‑Malo) for water quality and safety.[^6][^8]
- Combine beaches integrated into lively towns (Saint‑Malo, Carnac, Perros‑Guirec) with one or two quieter coves (Men Du, Moulin beach, smaller Morbihan coves) to balance stimulation with calm days.[^2][^3][^10]
- For a child who may be sensitive to cold water, focus swim‑intensive days on Emerald Coast and Morbihan/Quiberon Bay, where temperatures and shelter are generally more favourable.[^5][^10]
### Logistical tips (parking, access, timing)
- Many of the recommended beaches have car parks very close to the sand (Binic/Étables, Morbihan Gulf, Quiberon Peninsula), which reviewers underline as a key advantage when travelling with kids and beach gear.[^3][^10]
- Plan main swimming windows around mid‑tide to high tide at paddling‑pool beaches (Men Du, La Banche, Casino/Châtelet), and reserve low‑tide windows for rock‑pooling and shellfish exploration with buckets and nets.[^2][^9][^3]
- Use local tourism websites and webcam services (e.g. Carnac, Quiberon Bay) for up‑to‑date information on swimming conditions, lifeguard hours and tide states before heading out.[^6][^11]
### How these beaches fit a two‑week Nantes–Brittany itinerary
Given a 14‑day trip via Nantes by car or mixed transport, you could reasonably include:

- Emerald Coast block (Saint‑Malo, Dinard, Saint‑Briac) for Sillon, Bon‑Secours and La Salinette, plus historical towns.[^2][^11]
- Pink Granite Coast block (Perros‑Guirec area) for Trestraou/Saint‑Guirec and coastal walks among the granite formations.[^4][^14]
- Morbihan/Quiberon block (Carnac, La Trinité, possibly Gulf of Morbihan islands) for Grande Plage de Carnac, Kervillen, Men Dû and calm coves.[^6][^10]
- Optional Bay of Saint‑Brieuc block (Binic, Saint‑Quay‑Portrieux, Rosaires) if you prefer more low‑key local beaches with good children’s infrastructure between the main tourist hubs.[^3]

This balance would give your family high‑quality, varied impressions of Breton coastlines without turning the holiday into a race between too many spots.

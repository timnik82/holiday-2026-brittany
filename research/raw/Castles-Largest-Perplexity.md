# best and largest castles of Brittany, France for a family. Rank by the area you can actually visit inside the castle with expositions and or museums

The best options are the big “museum castles” like the Château des ducs de Bretagne in Nantes, Kerjean, Josselin, Fougères, Suscinio, Vitré and Dinan, which together offer the largest amount of indoor space you can actually walk through with exhibitions and museums in or near Brittany.[^1_1][^1_2][^1_3][^1_4][^1_5][^1_6][^1_7][^1_8][^1_9][^1_10][^1_11]
If you include historical Brittany beyond the current regional boundary, the clear number one by indoor museum area is the Nantes castle; within present‑day Brittany, Kerjean and Josselin are the strongest for large, furnished interiors plus dedicated museum spaces.[^1_3][^1_12][^1_8][^1_10][^1_1]

## 1. Château des ducs de Bretagne (Nantes)

Nantes’ castle is historically the seat of the Dukes of Brittany and today houses the city’s main history museum inside 32 rooms, making it the single largest coherent indoor museum circuit associated with Breton history.[^1_1][^1_3]
Beyond the interiors, you can stroll around a 1.5‑hectare fortress with ramparts, courtyard and moat gardens, which gives plenty of “off‑museum” space for younger children to run without leaving the site.[^1_3][^1_1]

## 2. Château de Kerjean

Château de Kerjean explicitly advertises a tour of 25 rooms, some equipped with original multimedia installations, which implies a sizeable proportion of the building is open to visitors in a structured route.[^1_10]
The site adds a chapel and a collection of Breton furniture, so the indoor visit is substantial and varied, while remaining compact enough for a family to cover in a half‑day.[^1_10]

## 3. Château de Josselin

Josselin’s self‑guided visit gives access to the ground floor rooms, while the guided tour adds state rooms plus part of the more intimate first floor, resulting in a fairly large interior circuit compared with many Breton castles.[^1_8]
You then continue into the 3‑hectare landscaped gardens and finish at the Doll and Toy Museum housed in the former stables, which is a dedicated museum space that tends to be a highlight for children.[^1_12][^1_8]

## 4. Château de Fougères

Château de Fougères is often described as the largest medieval fortified castle in Europe, and while much of that area is ramparts, towers and courtyards, a modern visitor circuit combines indoor exhibition spaces with extensive accessible fortifications.[^1_2][^1_4]
Audio guides and guided visits lead you through towers and walls with historical commentary, so although the indoor museum rooms themselves are fewer than at Nantes, the total accessible built area is huge and engaging for families who enjoy exploring.[^1_16][^1_4]

## 5. Château de Suscinio

At Suscinio, reconstructed décor immerses you in the daily life of Anne of Brittany, and multiple rooms are dressed with furniture and scenography rather than just bare stone, so you spend a meaningful amount of time indoors.[^1_9]
The “logis merveilleux” west wing offers an immersive retelling of Arthurian and other Breton legends, and throughout the rooms children can try medieval strategy games, making this castle particularly strong in interactive, exhibition‑style content.[^1_5][^1_9]

## 6. Château de Vitré

Château de Vitré, residence of the Barons of Vitré, has housed a museum since 1877, now known as the Museum of Art and History, which occupies parts of the castle interior with sculptures, artefacts and historic rooms.[^1_6][^1_17]
Guided or self‑guided tours bring you through selected towers and rooms, and while the indoor circuit is shorter than Kerjean or Nantes, it combines well with exploring the well‑preserved medieval town.[^1_17][^1_6]

## 7. Château de Dinan (keep)

The Dinan castle complex includes a 14th‑century keep and gate linking into the town’s medieval ramparts, and the keep itself is arranged as a museum with multiple “elegant rooms” on several levels.[^1_7][^1_11]
Visitors can explore the kitchen and an underground crypt, with exhibits and plaques explaining the historic uses of each space, so the indoor portion is more than just a single hall despite being smaller than the top‑ranked castles.[^1_11][^1_7]

## 8. Domaine de la Bourbansais

La Bourbansais consists of a historic château, formal gardens and a large zoo; the overall estate covers almost 100 hectares, but the bulk of this area is outdoor animal enclosures rather than indoor museum rooms.[^1_13]
The château and gardens can be visited, sometimes with guided explanations, while the zoo, play areas, plant maze and bird/dog shows make this one of the best “castle‑plus‑activity” sites for families, albeit not a top choice if your priority is interior museum surface.[^1_13]

## 9. Château de Rochefort‑en‑Terre

Rochefort‑en‑Terre’s castle is striking and set in scenic gardens, and it is used to host art exhibits and cultural events, giving some indoor exhibition area but not a large permanent museum circuit comparable to Nantes or Kerjean.[^1_15]
For a family, the appeal is the combination of a small‑scale castle interior, gardens and the surrounding village, rather than spending hours inside continuous museum rooms.[^1_15]

## 10. Fort La Latte and other scenic fortresses

Fort La Latte is a famous clifftop fortress where most of the visit consists of crossing courtyards, climbing towers and reaching the keep; at the top you pass through a single museum room before accessing the 360° sea views.[^1_18][^1_14]
Other sites like Château de Tonquédec are primarily ruins with ramparts and towers to explore, offering limited indoor museum area but excellent scenery and short, adventurous visits that many children enjoy.[^1_19]

## Practical notes for choosing with a family

If maximizing indoor museum/exhibition time is your main criterion, prioritise Château des ducs de Bretagne (Nantes) and Kerjean for their explicitly large numbers of accessible rooms and structured museum layouts.[^1_1][^1_3][^1_10]
If you want a balance between substantial interiors and strong child appeal, Josselin (with the doll museum) and Suscinio (with immersive legends and games) are particularly well‑suited, while Fougères and Dinan add more “exploring towers and walls” to the mix.[^1_2][^1_4][^1_5][^1_12][^1_7][^1_8][^1_9][^1_11]

# Temporary Events and Le Voyage à Nantes Programme in Nantes, 7–9 August 2026

## Overview

This report compiles temporary events, exhibitions, installations, and family‑friendly activities in Nantes for 7–9 August 2026, with a focus on the Le Voyage à Nantes 2026 summer programme and the green line itinerary. It is based primarily on official organisers’ sites (Le Voyage à Nantes, Nantes Métropole) and major event calendars available as of June–July 2026; detailed daily timetables for some events are still pending publication and are noted accordingly.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The 2026 Summer Journey (Le Voyage à Nantes) runs from 4 July to 6 September 2026 and is organised around the theme “EARTH”, the first element in a four‑year cycle exploring earth, water, air and fire. During 7–9 August 2026 the full set of permanent artworks, most temporary installations, and the majority of partner exhibitions are operational and accessible.[^4][^1]

## Le Voyage à Nantes 2026: Structure and Practical Framework

### Dates, pass system and general access

Le Voyage à Nantes 2026 (“L’événement estival du Voyage à Nantes” / “The Summer Journey”) is scheduled from 4 July to 6 September 2026, with installations and exhibitions distributed along and around the green line through central Nantes. The green line is a roughly 20 km painted trail on the ground connecting around 130 permanent artworks and a large set of temporary works, stretching from downtown to the Loire riverbanks and wider metropolitan sites; it can be explored year‑round, but the density of ephemeral works is highest in summer.[^2][^5][^1][^4]

An event listing updated 5 June 2026 describes a specific “Pass Voyage” with tariffs for 24 h, 48 h, 72 h and 7 days (30 €, 40 €, 49 €, 75 € respectively) sold via the official online ticketing; it is presented as necessary to access certain animations of Le Voyage à Nantes, though many outdoor artworks and some partner events remain free‑access. In parallel, a broader Pass Nantes city card is advertised on the Voyage site for museums, transport and attractions; precise 2026 inclusions and pricing are to be confirmed in the dedicated Pass Nantes section when the summer programme is fully published in May–June.[^1][^2][^4]

### Thematic focus “EARTH” and artistic concept

The 2026 edition opens a four‑year cycle devoted to the elements, starting with “EARTH” as the first theme entrusted to nine contemporary artists. Curatorial texts emphasise earth both as deep matter and metaphorical force, containing the memory of the city and symbolising the rebirth of life, while also being a fragile vessel in cosmic space; artists are invited to interpret this across diverse media and spatial interventions.[^4][^1]

According to Le Voyage’s general director, the goal is to let art, nature and the city be shared experiences, accessible to all and structuring different scales of perception in urban space. This translates practically into artworks installed in squares, gardens, castle moats, cathedral crypts and other familiar and unexpected sites, many of which are on or near the green line and are easily walkable for families.[^1]

### Green line itinerary and year‑round elements

The green line itself is a core device: a painted line on the ground that visitors follow to discover artworks, museums, gardens and viewpoints across roughly twenty kilometres. It connects major sites such as the Château des ducs de Bretagne, Île de Nantes, Jardin des Plantes, Graslin district, and extends towards the Loire estuary and the vineyards via complementary itineraries (Estuaire Nantes–Saint‑Nazaire, journey in the vineyards, Traversée Bretonne).[^5][^1]

Nantes Métropole describes more than 130 permanent artworks located from city centre to ocean, estuary and vineyards, including artist‑designed hotel rooms, shop signs, playgrounds and large public sculptures; each summer around sixty new ephemeral works are added, some of which later become permanent, enriching the collection.[^6][^1]

## Family‑Friendliness and Estimated Visit Durations

### Child appeal of main Voyage à Nantes elements

For a family with a 10‑year‑old child, several Voyage à Nantes components stand out as particularly engaging:

- **Pierrick Sorin at Jardin des Plantes**: Sorin’s installations typically use playful visual illusions and humour, and are sited in the botanical garden where children can also explore themed areas and unusual plant sculptures. Estimated visit: 1–1.5 h including garden walk.[^6][^1]
- **Barbara Schroeder at Jardin Extraordinaire**: This terraced garden above the Loire offers dramatic views, waterfalls and exotic plants; the Schroeder work adds an artistic layer to a physically stimulating environment with stairs and paths. Estimated visit: 1–1.5 h.[^1]
- **Louis Guillaume in the Château moats + castle museum**: Combining the EARTH‑themed artwork in the moat with a visit to the Château des ducs de Bretagne museum (history of Nantes) gives both outdoor and indoor experiences. Estimated visit: 2–3 h (castle + moat + courtyard).[^7][^1]
- **Théo Mercier at Place Graslin and surrounding district**: The piece itself is a quick stop, but the theatre square and surrounding streets make for a pleasant stroll. Estimated visit: 30–45 min.[^1]
- **Anne‑Charlotte Finel in cathedral crypts + cathedral**: Potentially more atmospheric and slightly darker; children interested in mystery, architecture and video art may find it compelling. Estimated visit: 45–60 min.[^6][^1]
- **Mauves Balnéaire**: A pseudo‑beach on the Loire with games and relaxation, enabling a “river beach” afternoon combined with picnic; good if the family wants water‑adjacent downtime without driving to the coast. Estimated visit: 2–3 h.[^4][^1]

These durations assume moderate walking pace and time for breaks; they can be combined with other stops (markets, cafés, playgrounds) to create 2–3 major activities per day.

### Museums and exhibitions for mixed adult/child interest

Museums and exhibitions that balance adult art‑historical content with child engagement include:

- **Musée d’arts de Nantes (Odyssée de l’oubli, Ce que la terre retient)**: Offers a substantial permanent collection plus temporary exhibitions; children can be engaged through specific artworks and interactive mediation tools. Estimated visit: 2 h.[^7][^1]
- **Musée Jules Verne**: Focused on the writer’s life and works; the theme of adventure, travel and science fiction tends to attract children 8–12, and location on a hill above the Loire adds scenic value. Estimated visit: 1–1.5 h.[^3][^1]
- **Natural History Museum (“expos hors les murs”)**: Traditional natural‑history displays (animals, minerals) plus outdoor or off‑site exhibits integrated into Voyage à Nantes; strong family appeal. Estimated visit: 1.5–2 h for main building.[^6][^1]

Exhibitions like Expression(s) décoloniale(s) #4 or Constant Struggle are more discursive and may be better suited to adults; they can be visited while the child enjoys nearby outdoor installations.[^7][^1]
